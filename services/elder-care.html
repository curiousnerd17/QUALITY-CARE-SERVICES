What's included

What Elder Care Includes

Elder Care is day-to-day support for an older parent living at home. It is practical help and company, not medical treatment.

Personal care and daily routine

  • Help with bathing, dressing, grooming and hygiene
  • Support moving around the house and getting in and out of bed safely
  • Meals served on time and help with eating where needed
  • Reminders for medicines your parent already takes

Company and supervision

  • Someone present through the day, or overnight where that is the need
  • Conversation and company for a parent who is alone most of the day
  • Keeping an eye on how your parent is doing and telling you what changes
  • Light tidying around your parent, so the room stays liveable
  • Relief for a family member who has been managing alone

What this service does not cover

  • This is not nursing or medical care — if that is needed, it is Patient Care
  • We do not diagnose, prescribe, or decide anything about medication
  • We are not an old-age home; this is support in your parent's own house
  • Full household work is arranged separately, not folded into elder care duty

Why we state this plainly

Knowing where our work stops means nobody discovers a gap on the first day. If your parent's needs have moved past daily support into medical care, we will say so on the call and move you to the right service — including when that is not one of ours.

Who it's for

Who Elder Care Is For

Most families who call us about elder care are in one of these situations.

A parent living alone

They manage most things, but the day has become harder — and the family would rather someone was there than wait for a fall to prove the point.

A parent who has become unsteady

Walking, bathing and stairs now carry real risk, and the family wants someone there for the parts of the day where a fall is most likely.

A family that is out at work all day

The house is empty from morning to evening, and nobody is comfortable leaving an elderly parent on their own for that stretch.

Adult children living outside Kota

You cannot be here to check in, and you want someone reliable at the house plus a local team you can actually reach when you have a question.

Not sure whether you need Elder Care or Patient Care?

Elder Care is ongoing daily support for an older parent who is generally well. Patient Care is driven by a condition — illness, recovery after hospital, or being bedridden. If there is a medical situation rather than simply age, Patient Care usually fits better.

What to expect

How Arranging Elder Care Works

Five stages, told from your side, so you know what you are agreeing to before you agree to it.

1 · First contact

Call or message and tell us about your parent — how they are managing, where you are in Kota, and what part of the day worries you most.

2 · We listen first

We ask what your parent can still do alone, what has become difficult, and which hours matter — then tell you what level of support actually fits.

3 · Matching the right person

We suggest verified caregivers with experience of elderly households and share their details. You decide who you are comfortable with.

4 · Scheduling and start

We agree the hours, make expectations clear on both sides, and the caregiver starts — usually with a short settling-in period so your parent gets used to them.

5 · Ongoing support

We stay reachable while the service runs. Needs change slowly with age — tell us when they do and we will adjust the arrangement.

Transparency

How Pricing Works

We do not publish rates, because a number set before we know your parent would not be an honest one. What we can do is tell you exactly what the charge depends on.

  • The level of support — company and supervision, or hands-on help with hygiene and mobility.
  • Duration — hours per day, days per week, and how long the arrangement is expected to run.
  • Shift pattern — day, night, 12-hour or 24-hour duty, and whether one person or two in shifts.
  • Specific requirements — what your parent's situation actually asks of the person doing the work.

We discuss the exact charge with you once we understand the situation, and there is no obligation to go ahead after that conversation.

Common questions

Elder Care — Common Questions

The things families ask us on the phone, answered here so you do not have to.

What is the difference between Elder Care and Patient Care?

Elder Care is ongoing daily support for an older parent who is generally well but finds the day harder than they used to — hygiene, moving around, meals, and company. Patient Care is driven by a medical situation: recovery after hospital, illness, or a parent who has become bedridden. If you are not sure which one your parent needs, describe the situation on the phone and we will tell you honestly which fits.

Can the caregiver stay overnight or for 24 hours?

Yes. We arrange day support, overnight support, and 12-hour or 24-hour duty. For 24-hour care we can arrange either a single caregiver with rest breaks or two caregivers working in shifts, depending on what your parent needs and what the household can accommodate. Shorter daily hours are equally possible where that is genuinely enough.

Will the caregiver do household work as well?

The caregiver's time is meant for your parent, so the duty covers light tidying around them — not the household's full cleaning, laundry and cooking. Families who need both usually arrange elder care alongside Maid Services, Home Cook Services or Housekeeping, so neither job is done at the expense of the other. We will tell you plainly which combination fits rather than stretch one person across everything.

My parent does not want a stranger in the house. What then?

This is one of the most common things families tell us, and it is a fair objection. We suggest starting with fewer hours so your parent meets the caregiver rather than has one imposed on them, and we try to match on temperament and language, not only on skill. If the person is not a fit after a genuine try, tell us and we will arrange someone else.

Are your elder care staff verified?

Yes. All staff go through a verification process before placement, including identity verification, background checks, and an assessment of their experience and suitability for the specific duty. We share relevant details with the family before the service begins, because we know they will be spending long hours alone with your parent.

How are elder care charges decided?

Charges depend on four things: the level of support needed, how many hours per day and days per week, the shift pattern (day, night, 12-hour or 24-hour), and any specific requirements of your parent's situation. We discuss the exact charge with you after we understand the situation, with no obligation to proceed. We do not publish rates on this page because a number set without knowing your parent would not be an honest one.
