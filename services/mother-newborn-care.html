What's included

What Mother & Newborn Care Includes

This is combined support for the first weeks after delivery — looking after the mother's recovery and the baby's daily care at the same time. It is experienced, practical help at home, not medical treatment.

Support for the mother

  • Help so the mother can rest and recover, not run the house
  • Warm meals on time and a hand with the daily routine
  • Support with feeding — positioning, routine and comfort
  • An experienced, steadying presence through the day or night

Care for the newborn

  • Gentle bathing, changing and nappy care
  • Feeding support and help settling the baby to sleep
  • Keeping the baby's things clean, ready and to hand
  • A watchful, experienced eye and calm reassurance for new parents
  • Relief for a family stretched thin by broken nights

What this service does not cover

  • This is not medical or nursing care — we do not replace your doctor, gynaecologist or paediatrician
  • We do not diagnose, prescribe, or decide anything about the mother's or baby's treatment
  • We are not a maternity home or hospital; this is support in your own home
  • Full household work is arranged separately, not folded into mother & newborn duty

Why we state this plainly

Knowing where our work stops means nobody discovers a gap on the first day. If mother or baby needs medical attention, that belongs with your doctor — we will say so plainly and, where it helps, arrange Patient Care with a qualified nurse alongside.

What to expect

How Arranging Mother & Newborn Care Works

Five stages, told from your side, so you know what you are agreeing to before you agree to it.

1 · First contact

Call or message and tell us the basics — the due date or delivery date, where you are in Kota, and whether you need day support or someone through the night.

2 · We listen first

We ask how the delivery went, how the mother is recovering, and what worries you most about the baby — then tell you what level of support actually fits.

3 · Matching the right person

We suggest verified caregivers experienced with newborns and post-delivery mothers, and share their details. You decide who you are comfortable having in the house.

4 · Scheduling and start

We agree the hours — day, night or 24-hour — make expectations clear on both sides, and the caregiver starts, ideally a little before or right after you come home.

5 · Ongoing support

We stay reachable while the service runs. The early weeks change quickly — tell us as the baby settles or needs shift, and we will adjust the arrangement.

Transparency

How Pricing Works

We do not publish rates, because a number set before we know your situation would not be an honest one. What we can do is tell you exactly what the charge depends on.

  • The level of support — care mainly for the baby, mainly for the mother's recovery, or both together through the day.
  • Duration — hours per day, days per week, and how many weeks of support you expect to need.
  • Shift pattern — day, night or 24-hour support, and whether one person or two where nights are covered separately.
  • Specific requirements — a caesarean recovery, twins, or any particular routine you want kept.

We discuss the exact charge with you once we understand the situation, and there is no obligation to go ahead after that conversation.

Common questions

Mother & Newborn Care — Common Questions

The things families ask us on the phone, answered here so you do not have to.

What is the difference between Mother & Newborn Care and Child Care?

Mother & Newborn Care is for the first weeks after delivery — it supports the mother's recovery and the newborn together, through the day or overnight. Child Care is for looking after older children, usually while parents are at work. If your baby is very new and the mother is still recovering, this is the service that fits; if you mainly need someone for an older child, that is Child Care.

What does the caregiver actually do for the mother and the baby?

For the baby: gentle bathing, changing and nappy care, feeding support, and help settling the baby to sleep. For the mother: help so she can rest and recover — meals on time, a hand with the daily routine, and support with feeding. The idea is that the mother's real job is to heal and feed the baby, while an experienced person carries the rest of the day.

Can the caregiver stay for 24 hours or live in for the early weeks?

Yes. We arrange day support, overnight support, and 24-hour care for the early weeks — which is when many families most want someone through the night. For round-the-clock cover we can arrange either one person with proper rest breaks or two working in shifts, depending on what the household can accommodate. Shorter daily hours are equally possible where that is genuinely enough.

Is this medical or nursing care?

No. Mother & Newborn Care is experienced, practical support at home — it is not nursing and does not replace your doctor, gynaecologist or paediatrician. We do not diagnose, prescribe, or make decisions about the mother's or baby's medical treatment. If a clinical need comes up, it belongs with your doctor, and where it helps we can arrange Patient Care with a qualified nurse alongside.

Are your staff verified and experienced with newborns?

Yes. All staff go through a verification process before placement, including identity verification, background checks, and an assessment of their experience and suitability for the specific duty — here, real experience with newborns and post-delivery mothers. We share the relevant details with the family before the service begins, because we know this is a time you need to feel completely at ease about who is in the house.

How are mother & newborn care charges decided?

Charges depend on four things: the level of support needed, how many hours per day and days per week, the shift pattern (day, night or 24-hour), and any specific requirements such as a caesarean recovery or twins. We discuss the exact charge with you after we understand the situation, with no obligation to proceed. We do not publish rates on this page because a number set without knowing your situation would not be an honest one.
