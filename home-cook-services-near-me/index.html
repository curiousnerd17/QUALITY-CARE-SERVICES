The schedule

A Timetable That Only Just Works Will Not Last

This is the requirement most often arranged on a schedule with no room in it. It holds for a few weeks, then someone's hours change and the whole thing has to be renegotiated. The useful conversation at the start is not what time suits you today — it is how much the timing could move before the arrangement breaks.

  • How many meals a day, and whether they sit at opposite ends of it
  • How rigid each slot is — whether dinner at eight can become dinner at nine
  • Whether the kitchen is genuinely free at those hours or shared with the household's own cooking
  • Whether shopping is part of the arrangement, which adds a journey rather than an hour
  • How many people are being cooked for, and whether that number changes at weekends

Getting to the kitchen

What We Need Besides the Address

A kitchen is a room with a hundred small conventions nobody has ever had to explain. A few of them are worth saying out loud before anyone arrives.

A landmark near the house

A school, temple, market or main road nearby. Give it with the address rather than instead of it — this is a journey that gets made every day, in both directions.

How the cook gets in

Whether somebody will be home at that hour, or a key or a doorbell arrangement is needed. Worth settling before the first day rather than on it.

A location pin on WhatsApp

Faster than describing a route, and it removes the ambiguity that similar colony names sometimes create in Kota.

Whether the kitchen is free at that hour

Two people cooking in one kitchen at the same time is a different arrangement from one. If the household cooks as well, say so early.

What happens next

After You Call or Message

Four steps, and the first one is longer than households expect.

1 · We ask how your household eats

Not just the timings. What you actually eat in a normal week, who it is for, and what your kitchen already assumes.

2 · We match on taste, then on journey

Someone who cooks the way your household eats, and who can make the slot without the travel eating all the slack.

3 · Preferences are agreed before the first day

Meals, timings and anything your household keeps particular are settled in advance, so nobody is guessing in week one.

4 · Judge it over a fortnight

The first week is a stranger cooking in an unfamiliar kitchen for people whose preferences they are still learning. Give it longer than one meal.

Being clear

What We Cannot Do at Short Notice

  • Tell you on day one whether the food will suit your household — that takes a fortnight, not a meal
  • Advise on nutrition, diet plans or what anyone should be eating
  • Arrange cleaning, utensils, laundry or household chores under this service
  • Place anyone whose identity and background checks are not finished

Where a diet has come from a doctor or dietitian, give it to us in writing exactly as it was given to you. We pass it on as written; we do not interpret it.

Worth thinking about

Some Cooks Would Rather Cook Their Own Way

The constraint here is willingness, not skill or distance

This is the one placement where the person doing the work has an opinion about the result. A cook can be experienced, close by and available, and still be a poor match because your household wants less oil, or a particular cuisine, or a dish made the way your family has always made it. Some people would genuinely rather cook their own way, and that is not a fault — it is simply a mismatch worth finding before the first day rather than in month two. Proximity does nothing to help with it.

Before anyone arrives

Everyone We Place Is Verified First

  • Identity — government photo ID is checked against the person in front of us, so nobody is placed on a recommendation alone
  • Address — address proof is verified, so we know where the person lives before they are sent to the address you give us
  • Background and references — both completed before a placement is confirmed, never alongside it
  • Experience matching — someone who cooks well is not automatically someone who will cook to another household's taste, and this is the placement where that distinction decides it
  • Replacement — if the fit turns out to be wrong once someone has started, arranging a replacement is our job, not yours

Verification lowers the risk of the wrong person reaching your home; it is not a clinical credential. How we verify the people we place explains the full process.

Common questions

Questions Households Ask About the Practicalities

The ones that come up once a household starts working out whether the timings actually fit.

Can we change the meal times later if our routine changes?

Usually, but it is easier when there was room in the schedule to begin with. A pattern that only just worked at the start has nothing left to give when someone's office hours move or a child changes school, and that is when arrangements quietly fall apart. Tell us at the outset if your timings are likely to shift, and we will weight the match towards someone with room rather than someone who exactly fits today.

The cook will be alone in the house for part of the day. What should we settle before that starts?

Decide how they get in and who else holds a key, and settle the grocery arrangement in the same conversation — who buys, who decides, and how the money is recorded. Households that leave these to be worked out informally are the ones having an awkward conversation in month two. Write the answers down once and the question never comes up again.

If the cook also shops for us, does that change which areas work?

It can. Shopping ties the arrangement to a particular market as well as to your kitchen, and a route that runs house to market to house is a different proposition from a single journey. Tell us on the first call whether shopping is part of what you want, because it is easier to match for it than to add it afterwards.

Our household eats a particular regional cuisine. Does that make this harder to arrange?

It narrows the list, which usually means the search takes a little longer rather than that it fails. Name the cuisine and a few dishes your household actually eats in a normal week — that is far more useful to us than a general description, and it is what we match against. Where the list is narrow, it is worth being flexible on area rather than on the cooking.

We are outside the areas you listed. Does a cook travel that far twice a day?

That is the honest sticking point for this service, and it is worth raising early. Where the arrangement covers both a morning and an evening meal, the cook is making two round trips rather than one, and a distance that looks reasonable on a map becomes four journeys a day in practice. Sometimes a single-meal arrangement works where a two-meal one does not. Tell us the location and which meals matter most.
