How often, per job

Not Everything Needs Doing at the Same Rate

Households tend to set one frequency for the whole arrangement — someone twice a week — and then wonder why certain things never seem to get done. Cleaning does not work at a single rate. Some of it is every visit, some is weekly, and some is monthly and therefore invisible until it has been missed for a quarter.

  • Which jobs belong to every visit — floors, surfaces, bathrooms, the kitchen area
  • Which are weekly rather than daily, and would be wasted effort done more often
  • Which are monthly or occasional, and how those get remembered when nothing prompts them
  • How many rooms each of those actually covers
  • Whether the visit is fixed in the week or can move by a day

Before the first morning

What to Settle Before the First Visit

Four decisions. Making them once removes almost everything that otherwise gets improvised on the doorstep.

Something recognisable to steer by

A well-known building or junction close to you. Written addresses in Kota are often approximate; a fixed point that anybody in the area can name is not.

How they get in, and when

Whether somebody is home, or a key arrangement is needed, and whether the timing is fixed or has room in it. Three answers, decided once.

A location pin on WhatsApp

One tap, no typing, and no chance of arriving at a colony with almost the same name as yours somewhere else in the city.

Where supplies are kept

Who provides them, where they live, and who says when something has run out. A small thing that causes a surprising amount of friction when left unsaid.

What happens next

After You Call or Message

Four steps, and the first is the one that determines whether the rest holds.

1 · We ask what finished looks like

Room by room, job by job. Not how clean you want it — what a completed version of each job actually looks like in your house.

2 · We ask which rooms come first

Your hierarchy, in your order. It is invisible from outside and it is the most common reason good work still disappoints.

3 · You see the details before the first visit

Who is coming and what has been checked, before the arrangement starts rather than on the morning it does.

4 · Show the house once, properly

Thirty minutes walking through it prevents most of what a new person otherwise gets wrong in the first fortnight.

Being clear

What We Cannot Do at Short Notice

  • Work to a standard that exists only in somebody's head — "thorough" is not a specification, and nobody can be held to it
  • Promise a standard of result, which is not ours to promise on another person's behalf
  • Arrange deep cleaning or sanitisation, which is a different job from regular cleaning
  • Place anyone whose identity and background checks are not finished

The first two are connected. Where the standard is written down, the work can be discussed factually; where it is not, every conversation about it becomes a complaint about a person.

Worth thinking about

Here It Is the Standard, Not the Distance

This is the arrangement where the result gets looked at every time

For general household help we would tell you that a short journey genuinely counts, because reliability is most of what you are buying. Cleaning is the opposite case. The output here is inspected — you walk into the room afterwards and form a judgement — and households disagree profoundly about what a clean room is. Somebody living five minutes away who works to a different standard than yours will disappoint you every week, punctually. Get the standard written down first; then, and only then, let proximity break the tie.

Before anyone arrives

Everyone We Place Is Verified First

  • Identity — government photo ID is checked against the person in front of us, so nobody is placed on a recommendation alone
  • Address — address proof is verified, so we know where the person lives before they are sent to the address you give us
  • Background and references — both completed before a placement is confirmed, never alongside it
  • Experience matching — cleaning as the main job, done room by room, is not the same work as general everyday household help, and someone strong at one is not automatically suited to the other
  • Replacement — if the fit turns out to be wrong once someone has started, arranging a replacement is our job, not yours

Verification lowers the risk of the wrong person reaching your home; it is not a clinical credential. How we verify the people we place explains the full process.

Common questions

Questions About Setting the Standard

The ones that come up between deciding you want this and agreeing what it means.

How do we describe what "clean enough" means without using the word thorough?

Name the thing, not the adjective. "Floors mopped in all four rooms" can be checked by anyone; "floors kept thorough" cannot be checked by anybody, which is why arrangements built on it come apart. Go item by item and write what a finished version of each looks like. It takes twenty minutes once and settles the question permanently.

Some rooms matter more to us than others. Should we say so, or does that sound unreasonable?

Say so. Every household has a hierarchy and it is rarely obvious from outside — some care most about the kitchen, others about bathrooms, others about the living areas guests see. Nobody can guess yours, and a person working to the wrong one looks careless while doing exactly what was asked. Naming the order costs nothing and removes most of what would otherwise become friction.

Some jobs only need doing once a month. How do those not get forgotten?

Put a frequency against each item rather than against the arrangement as a whole. Every visit, weekly, monthly, occasionally — written down beside the task. The periodic ones are what slip, because nothing about a normal week reminds anyone they are due, and they are usually the jobs that are most noticeable once they have been missed for a quarter.

The work was good for the first month and then slipped. What do we do about that?

Revise the list rather than raise a complaint. Where the standard was written down at the start, this becomes a short factual conversation about a specific item, which is a very different thing from telling somebody their work has got worse. Naming that possibility in advance is what makes revising it routine. Tell us as well — we would rather hear it in month two than in month six.

We cannot tell whether we need housekeeping or a maid. Does one take longer to arrange than the other?

They are different jobs and they suit different people, so the search behaves differently for each. Rather than deciding from the labels, describe the work you actually want done — which rooms, what gets cleaned in each, and whether utensils, laundry and general chores are part of it. That settles the question in about a minute on a call, and it is the same information we would need from you anyway.
