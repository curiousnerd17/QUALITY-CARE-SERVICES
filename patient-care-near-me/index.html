Availability

How Soon Someone Can Start

Urgent requirements can usually be covered within 24 to 48 hours. A standard requirement typically takes 2 to 3 days, because matching the right person properly takes longer than filling a slot. Both figures reflect experience rather than a commitment — here is what moves them.

  • Whether a suitable person is free. Not simply whether anyone is free. A placement filled by the wrong person is a placement that fails in week two, so we would rather take an extra day than send someone unsuited to the condition.
  • The shift pattern you need. Day duty is generally quicker to fill than nights or 24-hour cover. Overnight placements draw on a smaller group of people and usually need more notice.
  • Where the house is. Caregivers live somewhere too. A placement near where a suitable person already lives is quicker to fill and far more likely to hold for months.
  • Whether nursing training is needed. Where the care genuinely requires a qualified nurse, the pool is smaller and the timeline is longer than for an experienced caregiver.
  • When you call. Our office hours are Monday to Saturday, 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM, and Sunday, 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM. A message sent at eleven at night is read when we next open, so for a discharge happening tomorrow, call today.

Getting to you

How We Find Your Address

Most families reach us mid-situation and are not thinking about addresses. You do not need a precise one. Any of these is enough to work with.

A landmark near you

A hospital, school, temple, market or main road near the house places you faster than a house number does. Name the one you would give an auto driver.

The hospital, for a discharge

If care starts the day someone comes home, the hospital and ward are often the most useful thing to tell us first — followed by where home actually is.

Your colony or locality

A locality name plus one nearby landmark resolves most addresses in Kota. If you know only one of the two, that is usually still enough.

A location pin on WhatsApp

If describing it is harder than showing it, send your location as a pin to our WhatsApp number. It is quicker than spelling out directions, particularly from a hospital corridor.

What happens next

After You Call or Message

Four things happen, in this order, so you know what you are waiting for at each point.

1 · We ask about the situation

The condition, the hours you need covered, your area, and when care needs to begin. This is also where we say if the situation needs something we do not provide.

2 · We check who is genuinely available

Against your hours, your area and the condition. If nobody suitable is free in your timeframe, you hear that rather than a holding answer.

3 · We share who is coming

The relevant details of the person, before the service begins — not on the day they arrive. You decide whether to go ahead.

4 · The placement starts

At the address and hours agreed, with the duty settled on both sides beforehand, so the first day is spent working rather than negotiating.

Being straight about it

What We Cannot Do at Short Notice

  • We cannot guarantee a same-day placement, and we will not say we can in order to hold your call
  • We cannot skip verification to move faster. It is the thing that makes the placement worth having
  • We are not an emergency service. If someone needs urgent medical help, call the emergency services or go to a hospital
  • Overnight and 24-hour cover at a few hours' notice is the hardest thing to arrange, and often is not possible

If any of this rules us out for tonight, that is genuinely useful to know now rather than at nine in the evening. Call anyway and we will tell you where you stand in a couple of minutes.

Worth knowing

Nearest Is Not the Same as Most Suitable

Proximity is a convenience, not a qualification

Searching for care near you is sensible — distance affects reliability, and someone who travels a long way each day is more likely to eventually stop coming. But the closest available person is not automatically the right one. Experience with an elderly parent who still moves around unaided is not the same as experience with a bedridden patient, and the difference matters far more than a few kilometres. We match on what the condition needs first, and on distance second.

Before anyone arrives

Everyone We Place Is Verified First

  • Identity — government photo ID is checked against the person in front of us, so nobody is placed on a recommendation alone
  • Address — address proof is verified, so we know where the person lives before they are sent to the address you give us
  • Background and references — both completed before a placement is confirmed, never alongside it
  • Experience matching — someone suited to a mobile patient needing company and reminders is not automatically suited to a bedbound patient needing turning and feeding
  • Replacement — if the fit turns out to be wrong once someone has started, arranging a replacement is our job, not yours

Verification lowers the risk of the wrong person reaching your home; it is not a clinical credential. How we verify the people we place sets out the full process.

Common questions

Questions About Reaching You

The practical things families ask when they are working out whether we can help where they live.

What if I am just outside Kota city limits?

Tell us the location and we will confirm before you plan around it. The limit is not a boundary on a map, it is whether a caregiver can travel there reliably every day without the journey itself becoming the reason a placement fails. If the answer is no, we would rather say so on the first call than accept the work and let it break in week two.

My relative is being discharged tonight. What can you realistically do?

Call us as early in the day as you can, because the hours between a discharge decision and the discharge itself are the ones we can actually use. If we can arrange someone in time, we will. If we cannot, we will tell you straight away so you can make other arrangements rather than waiting on us. We would rather lose the placement than be the reason nobody was there.

Does the charge change if I am further from your office?

Distance from our office is not a line item. What affects a charge is the level of care, the hours per day and days per week, the shift pattern, and the specific requirements of the patient's condition. Where a location is far enough that daily travel genuinely affects who is willing to take the placement, we will discuss it with you openly before anything is agreed.

Does the caregiver travel daily, or stay at the house?

Both arrangements exist and the right one depends on the shift. Day duty usually means travelling in and out. Night and 24-hour duty may involve staying, which means the household needs somewhere for that person to rest. Settle this before the placement starts rather than discovering it at ten at night on the first day.

I am arranging this from another city. Can you start before I arrive?

Yes, provided someone at the house can let the caregiver in and describe the daily routine. We will share the relevant details of the person coming before the service begins, so you know who is entering the home even if you are not there. Give us a second contact number in Kota — a relative or neighbour — so nothing waits on a call you may be unable to take.

Things we do not arrange

  • ICU or high-dependency setups at home
  • Physiotherapy, diagnostic tests or sample collection
  • Medical equipment supply, rental or maintenance
  • Ambulance or emergency medical response

Why we list them

These are real needs and they belong with hospitals, clinics, equipment suppliers and emergency services — not with us. Telling you now saves you a phone call. If you are unsure which of these your situation actually needs, call anyway and describe it; we will point you towards the right kind of provider even when that is not us.
