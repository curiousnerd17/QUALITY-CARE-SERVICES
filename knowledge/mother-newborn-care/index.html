Knowledge · Mother & Newborn Care

Support at Home After Delivery

The first few weeks after a baby arrives are the ones families are least prepared for — a mother recovering, a newborn who does not sleep on any schedule, and a household still expected to run. This section covers what practical help at home looks like in that period, and how to arrange it before you are too tired to think about it.

What this section covers

The Practical Questions, Answered Plainly

These are the things families ask us, usually in the last month of pregnancy or the first week home. Writing them down here does not replace that call — it just means you are not starting from nothing.

What we write about

  • What to arrange at home before the delivery date, so nothing is scrambled afterwards
  • What a post-delivery attendant actually does, hour to hour
  • Choosing between day support, overnight support and 24-hour cover
  • How long families typically arrange help for, and how to judge when to stop
  • Sharing a house with an attendant during a period with no privacy

What we will not write about

  • Feeding advice, latching technique, infant nutrition or weaning schedules
  • Post-delivery recovery, complications, wound care or anything clinical for mother or baby
  • Vaccination schedules, growth milestones, or when a symptom is serious

This is the category where the line matters most, so we will state it bluntly: we arrange practical support at home, and nothing on these pages is medical guidance for a mother or a newborn. For feeding, recovery, the baby's health or anything that worries you, speak to your doctor, paediatrician or a qualified medical professional. If it is an emergency, seek immediate medical help.

The service behind this section

What Mother & Newborn Care Means Here

Support for the mother's recovery alongside gentle, experienced care for the newborn through the early weeks. Every person we place is verified and briefed on the household before the first shift.

Real newborn experience, required

This is the one service where we do not treat experience as optional. An attendant placed here has genuinely done this work before with newborns and post-delivery mothers.

Two people to look after, not one

The mother's recovery matters as much as the baby's care. A placement that leaves her doing everything except holding the baby has not helped.

Arranged before you need it

Families who set this up before the delivery date have a far easier first week than families arranging it from a hospital bed. It is worth the early call.
