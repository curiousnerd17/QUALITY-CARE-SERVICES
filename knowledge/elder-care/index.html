Knowledge · Elder Care

Arranging Elder Care at Home

Elder care rarely starts with a decision. It starts with a series of small things — a fall, meals being skipped, a parent who has stopped going out — until the family realises the current arrangement is not holding. This section covers what to do about that, including the parts that are awkward to talk about.

What this section covers

The Practical Questions, Answered Plainly

These are the things families ask us on the first call, including the ones they apologise for asking. Writing them down here does not replace that call — it just means you are not starting from nothing.

What we write about

  • The signs that daily support is genuinely needed, rather than wanted
  • Raising it with a parent who does not want help, without taking their independence away
  • Hygiene and mobility support handled with dignity
  • Building a daily routine an attendant and a family can both keep to
  • Coordinating between siblings, including when they disagree

What we will not write about

  • Diagnosis, treatment, medication or dosing of any kind
  • Managing dementia, Parkinson's or any condition clinically — we can write about supporting daily life, not about treating an illness
  • Legal or financial advice on guardianship, property or inheritance

We arrange and supervise care at home; we are not a medical provider. For anything clinical — a diagnosis, a medication, a fall that needs assessing, or a change in behaviour that worries you — contact the treating doctor or a qualified medical professional. If it is an emergency, seek immediate medical help.

The service behind this section

What Elder Care Means Here

Reliable daily support for an elderly parent in their own home — hygiene, mobility, meals and companionship. Every person we place is verified and briefed on the specific household before the first shift.

Their home, their routine

The point of home-based elder care is that nothing has to be uprooted. We fit an attendant around the routine that already exists rather than imposing a new one.

Temperament matters most

Patience is the thing we weigh hardest for this service. Skill can be briefed; someone who will not rush an eighty-year-old cannot be.

Reachable while it runs

Our team is in Kota. If something needs sorting out mid-placement — for you, for your parent, or for the person on duty — you have someone local to call.

Or just ask

If you are trying to work out whether your parent needs daily help and what that would look like, a phone call will get you a straight answer in a few minutes. It costs nothing and there is no obligation attached to it.

Tell Us What the Situation Is

Describe it in your own words — how your parent is managing, what has changed, and the hours you think need covering. We will tell you honestly what kind of help fits, and if we are not the right people for it, we will say so.
