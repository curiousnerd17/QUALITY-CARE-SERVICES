Preparing Your Home Before a Housekeeping Visit

A cleaning visit produces far more when the house is ready for it and the priorities are known. This covers the practical preparation — clearing the way, showing the home, agreeing what matters most and settling access. It is about organising the visit, not about cleaning methods or products, which we deliberately do not advise on.

When this is useful

In the Days Before Someone Starts

Written for the household doing the arranging. If you have not yet worked out what should be on the list, creating a cleaning checklist is the companion piece.

  • A first visit is arranged and you want it to be productive
  • This is the first time someone else will clean your home regularly
  • Previous visits have left you feeling the time was not well used
  • Someone new is replacing a person who had been coming for a long time

What households should know

Clear the Way, Then Show the House

Four things. The first two take about half an hour between them and decide how much of the visit is spent on actual cleaning.

1 · Clear surfaces, do not tidy the house

This distinction matters and households routinely get it backwards. A cleaner cannot wipe a worktop covered in objects, so clearing surfaces converts directly into cleaning time. Tidying the whole house before someone arrives to clean it, on the other hand, is effort spent on the wrong thing. Ten minutes moving things off flat surfaces and picking things off floors is the highest-value preparation there is.

2 · The walk-through, in the house

Twenty minutes going room to room: what your household is particular about, which surfaces get missed, where supplies are kept, anything fragile that should be left alone. Almost everything a new person gets wrong in the first fortnight is something nobody showed them, and demonstrating once is far quicker than correcting three times.

3 · Say which rooms matter most

Every household has a hierarchy, and it is rarely obvious from outside. Some care most about the kitchen, others about bathrooms, others about visible living areas before guests arrive. Say yours plainly and give a priority order for a visit that runs short. Without one, the cleaner will choose an order — and it may not be the one you would have chosen.

4 · Settle access before the first morning

How they get in, what happens if nobody is home, and whether the timing is fixed. Decide this deliberately rather than improvising at the door. The hiring conversation is the natural place to agree it along with scope and frequency.

Practical considerations

The Practical Arrangements

Ordinary household organisation. Nothing here concerns cleaning products, methods, or any safety or environmental standard.

Supplies and equipment, agreed

Who provides them, where they are kept, and who says when something has run out. Whatever your household already uses is what should be available — this is a logistics question, not a recommendation.

Working equipment

Check that whatever will be used actually works before the first visit, and show how anything temperamental behaves. Someone fighting the equipment produces less in more time.

Access and timings

How they get in, whether the time is fixed, and what happens if the household is out. Settle it in advance rather than at the door on the first morning.

Which rooms are in the arrangement

Agreed at the start as a matter of scope. Both sides then know exactly what the visit covers, and nobody is left guessing where they are expected to be.

Anything fragile or particular

Point out what should be handled carefully or left alone entirely. Saying so once at the start is far better than the conversation that follows a breakage.

Water and somewhere to keep their things

A place to leave a bag and water within reach. Small, easily overlooked, and it signals that the arrangement is a professional one.

General guidance vs our service

Where This Guidance Ends and Our Service Begins

Everything above is general household organisation. This is the part our housekeeping placement is actually responsible for.

What Quality Care can help with

  • Agreeing the scope room by room with both sides before the first visit
  • Identity, address and background verification, completed before anyone enters your home
  • Telling you honestly when a scope is not realistic in the time proposed
  • Settling access, timings and frequency in advance
  • Being reachable locally if the arrangement needs adjusting

What we do NOT advise on

  • Cleaning products or chemicals. We do not name, recommend or advise on any product, nor on mixing, storing or handling them safely
  • Mould, pests, damp or any hazardous material. These need a qualified specialist, not a housekeeping arrangement
  • Health claims about cleaning, disinfection, or infection control
  • Cleaning methods or techniques for any surface or material
  • Pay, employment law or contracts — ask a qualified professional
  • Promising a standard of result. We agree a scope; we do not guarantee an outcome

We place people into homes and agree the scope with both sides before a visit starts. Anything involving products, chemicals, mould, pests, damp or a health concern belongs with a qualified specialist — and where a household has a patient at home, any specific hygiene requirement must come from the treating medical team, not from us.

Common questions

Questions Households Ask Us

Do we need to tidy before a cleaning visit?

Clearing surfaces is worth doing; tidying the whole house is not. A cleaner cannot wipe a surface covered in objects, so ten minutes clearing worktops, side tables and floors converts directly into more of the visit spent actually cleaning.

Who provides the cleaning supplies and equipment?

In most household arrangements the home provides them, but say so explicitly rather than assuming. Agree who buys, where things are kept, and who says when something has run out. Whatever your household already uses is what should be available.

Should we be at home for the first visit?

If you can, yes. A short walk-through showing the rooms, what your household is particular about and where things are kept saves far more time than it costs, and it is much harder to explain a house over the phone afterwards.

What if there is not enough time for everything?

Decide the priority order in advance rather than leaving it to be guessed. Say which rooms matter most on a short visit and what can wait until the next one. A cleaner working without a priority order will pick one, and it may not be yours.

Tell Us What Your Home Needs

Describe it in your own words — how many rooms, how often, and anything particular about the household. We will agree the scope with both sides before anyone starts, and if we are not the right people for it, we will say so.
