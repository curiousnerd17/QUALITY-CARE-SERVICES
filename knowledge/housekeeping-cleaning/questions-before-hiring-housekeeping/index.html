Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Housekeeping Service

"Clean" means something different in every household, which is why so many cleaning arrangements end in quiet disappointment on both sides. These are the questions worth asking before one is agreed — about verification, about scope, and about how the standard will actually be judged once someone is doing the work.

When this is useful

Before an Arrangement Is Agreed

A general checklist, whether you arrange through an agency or privately. Writing the scope down first makes this conversation much shorter — creating a cleaning checklist covers that.

  • Someone has been proposed and you want to check properly before agreeing
  • You are arranging regular cleaning for the first time
  • A previous arrangement disappointed and you cannot say exactly why
  • You are comparing options and cannot tell them apart

What households should know

Four Groups of Questions

Ask across all four. Households that only discuss frequency and hours are the ones most often disappointed by the result.

1 · Verification — before anything else

Yes-or-no questions deserving yes-or-no answers: has identity been verified, and against what document; has the current address been verified; has a background check been completed, and when; who is accountable if something goes wrong. Be clear with yourself about what this establishes — who someone is, and that nothing adverse is on record. It is not a character reference, and nobody can guarantee that part.

2 · Scope — never use the word "thorough"

This is where the service succeeds or fails. Go room by room and surface by surface rather than relying on a word that means something different to everyone. Ask directly whether the scope you have described is realistic in the time being proposed, and treat an honest "no, not in ninety minutes" as the most useful answer in the whole conversation.

3 · Frequency and what changes between visits

Most households need some things every visit and others occasionally. Ask how that is normally handled: which tasks are routine, which are periodic, and how the periodic ones get remembered. An arrangement where everything is nominally in scope every time usually means the occasional things quietly never happen.

4 · How the standard will be judged

Agree in advance how you will say something was missed and how the person will say something could not be done in the time. Both directions matter. Arrangements where only the household can raise things tend to end without anyone having said why, and the reasons were usually fixable.

Practical considerations

Answers Worth a Second Question

None of these means someone is unsuitable. All of them mean ask once more before agreeing.

"Everything, thoroughly"

A long scope agreed instantly, with no questions about the time available, usually means it has not been thought through. The disappointment still arrives; it just arrives later.

Claims about health or hygiene benefits

An agency claiming a cleaning arrangement will make a home healthier or germ-free is asserting something it cannot substantiate. Cleaning is cleaning; health claims belong elsewhere.

Offers to handle mould, pests or damp

These are specialist problems and are not housekeeping. Anyone offering to deal with them as part of a cleaning visit is taking on something that needs a qualified specialist.

"We can start today, no paperwork"

Verification takes time by definition. An arrangement that skips it has skipped the part that protects the household.

Vagueness about accountability

"We will sort it out" is not an answer. You want to know who to call, in what timeframe, and what happens if a replacement is needed at short notice.

No questions about your home

Someone intending to do this well will ask how many rooms, how the house is used, and what your household is particular about. Silence there is a signal.

Common questions

Questions Households Ask Us

What verification should be complete before someone starts?

Identity proof, address proof and background checks, completed before the first visit rather than during the first week. Ask specifically what was checked and when. What that establishes is who someone is and that nothing adverse is on record — not a statement about how they will work.

How do we agree what thorough actually means?

Go through it room by room rather than using the word. Which rooms, which surfaces, and how often each is done. Thorough means something different in every household, and it is the single most common source of disappointment on both sides.

How often does a home actually need cleaning?

It depends on the size of the home, how many people live in it and how it is used, so we would rather not give a general answer. Working through what genuinely needs doing and how often usually settles the frequency in ten minutes.

How should we raise it when something has been missed?

Directly, early and specifically, referring back to the agreed scope rather than to a general impression. Most shortfalls in the first weeks are misunderstandings about what a task included, and they correct quickly when raised before frustration accumulates.
