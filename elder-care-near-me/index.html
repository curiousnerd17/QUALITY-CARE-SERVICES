How soon, and how long

Starting Is Easier Than Lasting

Most elder care requirements are not emergencies, so the start date is usually the easy part. The harder question — the one that decides whether you are arranging this once or three times — is what the arrangement asks of the person doing it, every day, for months.

  • Whether you need a few hours a day, a full day, night support, or someone living in
  • How far the caregiver travels to reach your parent, repeated daily rather than once
  • Whether your parent needs help at fixed hours or unpredictably through the day
  • How specific the requirement is — a particular language, a particular kind of experience, a particular temperament
  • Whether anyone else is at home during the day, or the caregiver is the only adult present

Getting to the house

How We Find Your Parent's Address

You do not need a formal address. Any of these is enough for us to work out where your parent lives and who can reach them.

A landmark nearby

A school, temple, market or main road close to the house. In most of Kota this locates a home faster than a written address does.

The colony or locality

The colony name and a crossing close to it is usually enough. If you are unsure of the exact colony, describe what is around the house instead.

A location pin on WhatsApp

WhatsApp can share a location as a pin. If describing the way is difficult, sending the pin to our number is the simplest option.

Someone who can guide on the day

A neighbour, relative or the parent themselves who can answer a phone and direct someone the last few streets. Give us that number as well as yours.

What happens next

After You Call or Message

Four steps, in this order.

1 · We ask what your parent's day looks like

Not a list of tasks — the shape of the day. When they wake, what they can still do themselves, where they need a hand, and what they would find intrusive.

2 · We look for someone who can keep the journey up

Suitability first, then whether the travel is realistic over months. A person who fits perfectly but cannot get there reliably is not a match.

3 · You get their details before anyone arrives

You know who is coming to your parent's home, and what their background is, before the arrangement starts rather than after.

4 · The first two weeks decide it

Settling in takes longer for an elderly parent than most families expect. Tell us early if something is not right — that is when it is easiest to fix.

Being clear

What We Cannot Do at Short Notice

  • Place anyone whose identity and background checks are not finished
  • Match a narrow combination of language, experience and area immediately
  • Provide nursing or clinical care under elder care — that is Patient Care, and it is arranged differently
  • Promise that one named person will still be the caregiver years from now

Elder care is worth arranging properly rather than quickly. If you need someone this week we will say what is realistic for this week.

Worth thinking about

The Right Person Matters More Than the Short Journey

Your parent has to accept this person, not just you

Many families arrive at elder care after months of a parent insisting they do not need help. In that situation the caregiver who lives closest is not the one who makes it work — the one your parent will actually let into the room is. Temperament, patience and the language your parent is most comfortable in decide whether an arrangement survives the first fortnight. A slightly longer journey is a small price for a person your parent stops arguing with.

If you are weighing what your parent actually needs, our elder care guides go through the signs worth noticing and the questions worth asking.

Before anyone arrives

Everyone We Place Is Verified First

  • Identity — government photo ID is checked against the person in front of us, so nobody is placed on a recommendation alone
  • Address — address proof is verified, so we know where the person lives before they are sent to the address you give us
  • Background and references — both completed before a placement is confirmed, never alongside it
  • Experience matching — someone patient with a parent who asks the same question three times is not automatically the right fit for a parent who needs physical help to stand
  • Replacement — if the fit turns out to be wrong once someone has started, arranging a replacement is our job, not yours

Verification lowers the risk of the wrong person reaching your parent; it is not a clinical credential. How we verify the people we place explains the full process.

Common questions

Questions Families Ask About Elder Care

The things that come up once a family starts working out whether this will actually hold.

Will the same caregiver keep coming, or will it change every few weeks?

The intention is always one person who stays, because an elderly parent settling in with someone new every month is the opposite of what elder care is for. What breaks continuity is usually distance — a caregiver who finds the daily journey too long stops being reliable long before they say anything. That is why we would rather place someone whose travel is sustainable than someone who is simply free this week.

My parent says everything is fine even when it is not. How do I find out what is actually happening?

Parents under-report. They do not want to worry their children, and they will often say the arrangement is fine rather than admit that something is not working. Ask us instead of asking only your parent, and ask early — a small mismatch raised in week one is easy to correct, and the same mismatch raised in month four has usually already done its damage.

My parent's day runs to a fixed routine. Can the caregiver come at the same time each day?

A fixed daily time is easier to hold when the caregiver lives within a short, predictable journey of your parent. Tell us the times that actually matter — when your parent wakes, eats and takes medication — and we will work out whether a particular person can meet them consistently rather than most days. Routine is not a small detail for an elderly person; a caregiver arriving at a different hour each day can unsettle the whole arrangement.

What if my parent refuses the caregiver after a few days?

It happens, and it is not a reason to give up on the arrangement. Sometimes the fit is genuinely wrong, and arranging a replacement is our job rather than yours. Sometimes it is simply that your parent has never had help before and needs longer than a few days. Tell us which one it looks like from your side and we will tell you what we are seeing from ours.

We are thinking about this for later, not right now. Is it worth calling?

Yes, and earlier is easier. Families usually think about elder care for months before they arrange it, and the calls that go worst are the ones made in a hurry after a fall. A conversation now costs nothing, puts you under no obligation, and means that when you do need someone we already know your parent's area, routine and requirement.
