How far ahead

Arranging Against a Due Date

This is the one requirement families bring to us before it exists. That is an advantage, and it is worth using — but it also means the conversation is about a window rather than a day, and about a household that has not happened yet.

  • Whether you want someone from the day mother and baby come home, or a little after
  • Whether the need is days, nights, or both — and which of the two you are least able to cover yourselves
  • Roughly how many weeks you expect the arrangement to run
  • Who else will be in the house, and what they will already be doing
  • How narrow the experience requirement is — newborn specifically, rather than children generally

Getting to the house

Which House We Should Be Reaching

Worth settling early, because it is the one detail families most often assume we already know.

The house mother and baby will actually stay in

Many families spend the early weeks at a parent's or in-law's home rather than their own. Tell us which address the arrangement is for, and whether that is likely to change part-way through.

A landmark nearby

A school, temple, market or main road close to that house. Give it alongside the address rather than instead of it — whoever is coming will be arriving somewhere they have never been, often early in the morning.

A location pin on WhatsApp

Sending a pin to our number takes a few seconds and removes the guesswork entirely. In a household that is already stretched, it is usually the least effortful way to answer this.

Someone other than the mother to call

Give us a second contact in the household. In the first weeks the mother is the person least able to answer a phone, and that is exactly as it should be.

What happens next

After You Call or Message

Four steps, and the order matters more here than usual because the start date is set before the requirement fully exists.

1 · We ask when, and where

The due date or the baby's age, the address the arrangement is for, and which hours you are least able to cover yourselves.

2 · We start from newborn experience

This requirement is filtered on experience first and location second, which is the opposite of how most placements work.

3 · You get their details before the start date

Not on the morning it begins. Where the date is weeks away, the details come closer to it rather than at the first call.

4 · The arrangement is reviewed once everyone is home

What a household needs on paper and what it needs in week one are rarely the same. Plan for a review rather than for being right first time.

Being clear

What We Cannot Do at Short Notice

  • Provide medical or nursing care for the mother or the baby — we are not a medical provider
  • Hold one named person against a date several weeks away and promise it will still be her
  • Place anyone whose identity and background checks are not finished
  • Tell you in advance exactly what your household will turn out to need

The first three are limits. The fourth is simply true of every household, and it is easier to work with once it is said out loud.

Worth thinking about

Experience With Children Is Not Experience With Newborns

This is the requirement where the shortlist narrows first

Supporting a newborn and a recovering mother at the same time is specific work, and someone who has looked after children generally has not necessarily done it. That distinction thins the list of suitable people before distance is even considered — which is why, for this service more than any other, the person who lives closest is frequently not on the list at all. If you have to choose between a shorter journey and someone who has done these particular weeks before, choose the experience.

Before anyone arrives

Everyone We Place Is Verified First

  • Identity — government photo ID is checked against the person in front of us, so nobody is placed on a recommendation alone
  • Address — address proof is verified, so we know where the person lives before they are sent to the address you give us
  • Background and references — both completed before a placement is confirmed, never alongside it
  • Experience matching — someone confident with a toddler is not automatically confident with a two-week-old baby and a mother who is still recovering from the delivery
  • Replacement — if the fit turns out to be wrong once someone has started, arranging a replacement is our job, not yours

Verification lowers the risk of the wrong person reaching your home; it is not a clinical credential. How we verify the people we place explains the full process.

Common questions

Questions Families Ask Before the Baby Arrives

The practical ones that come up while a household is still working out what it will need.

We have a due date, not a date. How far ahead should we arrange someone?

Earlier than most families expect. Talk to us while you are still planning rather than in the week the baby comes home, because the pool of people with real newborn experience is smaller than the pool of caregivers generally, and short notice thins it further. Tell us the due date and the area, and we will tell you what the picture looks like for that window.

The baby is already home and we are struggling. Is it too late to arrange help now?

No. Plenty of families only work out what they need once they are in the middle of it, and that is a normal way to arrive at this rather than a failure to plan. Call and describe the part of the day or night that is hardest right now. That is more useful to us than a tidy list of duties written when nobody has slept.

My mother or mother-in-law is already staying with us. Does that change what we need?

Often, yes — usually the shape rather than the amount. Where an experienced relative is already in the house, families more often want cover for the nights, or for the hours when that relative needs to rest, than someone present all day. Tell us who else is at home and what they are already doing, and the requirement usually becomes clearer in the conversation.

How much of this can we actually decide before the baby arrives?

Less than you would like, and that is worth accepting early. The area, the shift you think you want and the experience you need can all be settled in advance. How the household actually feels once mother and baby are home cannot. Treat the first arrangement as provisional with a review built in, rather than something that has to be exactly right the first time.

If we arrange someone weeks ahead, when do we find out who is coming?

You see the person's details before the service begins, not on the morning it starts. Where the start date is weeks away, that means the details come to you closer to the date rather than at the moment you first call — the checks are completed before a placement is confirmed, and confirming someone months early is not the same as confirming them properly.
