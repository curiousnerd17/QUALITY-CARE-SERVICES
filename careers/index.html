How hiring works

From Application to Joining

Seven stages, told from your side, so you know what happens after you apply.

1 · Application

You send us your details — the role you want, your experience, your area and the hours you can work.

2 · Initial screening

A short call to understand your experience and confirm the basics. We will tell you honestly if the role is not a fit.

3 · Interview

A proper conversation about the work you are comfortable with — and your chance to ask us questions before anything is agreed.

4 · Document verification

Identity, address and background checks. Every person we place goes through this, because we promise families exactly that.

5 · Selection

If it is a yes, we confirm the role, the hours and the pay with you. If it is a no, we say so rather than leave you waiting.

6 · Briefing, where the role needs it

Practical preparation for the specific placement, so you arrive knowing what is expected. We tell you clearly whether your role includes this.

7 · Joining

We introduce you to the family, confirm the task list on both sides, and stay reachable once the placement starts.

We would rather tell you the limits before you invest time in applying than have you find out afterwards. If a role is not right for you, we will say so.

Common questions

Questions Applicants Ask Us

The things people ask before applying, answered here so you do not have to call to find out.

Who can apply for work with Quality Care Services?

Anyone in or around Kota who wants to do this work honestly and can look after someone else's home or family member with care. You need valid identity and address proof, the ability to reach the areas we serve, and a willingness to go through our verification process. Formal qualifications matter less than reliability and temperament.

Do I need previous experience to apply?

For most roles, experience is preferred but not essential. Maid, housekeeping and cooking roles are open to people who have run a household well and can work to an agreed task list. Patient care is the exception: where a family needs clinical care we place a qualified nurse, so nursing roles do require the relevant qualification.

Is the work full-time or part-time?

Both. Many placements are part-time — a set number of hours or visits in the week. Others are full-time across the working day, and some care placements involve day or night shifts. Tell us on your application what hours you can genuinely commit to, and we will match you against work that fits.

Where would I be working?

In family homes across Kota — including Talwandi, Vigyan Nagar, Mahaveer Nagar, Kunhari and Landmark City, plus nearby areas. We try to match you to placements you can reach reasonably from where you live, so tell us your area on the application.

How is my pay decided?

Pay depends on the role, the hours, the shift pattern and what the placement actually involves. We agree the amount with you before you start — you will never be asked to begin work without knowing what you will be paid. We do not publish fixed rates on this page because a number set before we know the placement would not be an honest one.

Do I have to pay any fee to apply or to get placed?

No. There is no application fee, no registration fee, no training fee and no deposit of any kind. If anyone asks you for money in our name, do not pay it — call us directly on the number on this page and tell us.

What documents do I need?

Identity proof, address proof, and Aadhaar where you have it. If you have a police verification certificate, that helps. If you do not have one yet, say so on the application — it does not automatically stop you being considered, and we will explain what is needed for the role.

What does your verification process involve?

Identity verification, address verification, background checks, and an assessment of your experience against the specific duty. We do this because families are trusting us with a home and often with someone vulnerable, and we tell them plainly that every placement is verified. The same standard applies to everyone.

How long does the hiring process take?

It depends on how quickly we can complete screening and verification, and on whether a suitable placement is open when you apply. We will tell you honestly where you stand rather than leave you waiting without an answer. If nothing suitable is open, we keep your application on file for future openings.

Will I be interviewed?

Yes. After the initial screening call we arrange an interview so we can understand your experience, the kind of work you are comfortable with, and the hours you can commit to. It is also your opportunity to ask us questions before anything is agreed.

Do you provide training?

Where a role needs it, we brief and orient you before you start so you know what the placement expects. This is practical preparation for the specific duty rather than a formal certification course, and we will tell you clearly which applies to the role you are being considered for.

Can I choose which service I work in?

Yes — tell us the role you are applying for on the application. If we think your experience fits a different service better, we will discuss it with you rather than move you without asking. You are never placed into work you have not agreed to.

What happens if a placement is not working out?

Tell us. We stay reachable while a placement runs, for the family and for you. If the fit is wrong, we would rather resolve it or move you than have anyone stuck in a situation that is not working.

Is there any guarantee of work after I am selected?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. Being selected means you are verified and on our roster; actual placement depends on a family needing the service you provide, in an area you can reach, at hours you can work. We tell you what is realistic rather than promise volume we cannot hold.

How will you use the personal information on my application?

Only to assess your application, verify you, and contact you about work. We do not sell it or share it for advertising. Where we place you with a family, we share the relevant details they need to know about the person coming into their home. You can ask us what we hold about you, correct it, or ask us to delete it.

What we look for

The Qualities That Decide Who We Place

Skill can be taught and experience builds over time. These six are what we actually weigh when we decide whether to place someone in a family's home — and what a placement is reviewed against afterwards.

Compassion

Noticing that the person in front of you is unwell, tired, frightened or embarrassed — and adjusting how you work because of it. Most of the households we serve are having a difficult month.

Professionalism

Arriving prepared, keeping to the agreed task list, and raising a problem with us rather than letting it grow. You are doing skilled work, and we expect it to be carried out that way.

Reliability

Turning up when you said you would. A family planning around a shift cannot absorb a no-show, so we would rather you commit to fewer hours and keep every one of them.

Respect

Treating the home as someone's private space and the household as people, not a task. This runs both ways: we ask the same of every family before a placement begins.

Integrity

Telling the truth about your experience, your availability and anything that goes wrong. We would far rather hear an uncomfortable fact early than discover it later.

A patient-first mindset

Putting the wellbeing of the person being cared for ahead of what is quickest or easiest that day, and knowing when a situation needs to be escalated to the family or to us.
